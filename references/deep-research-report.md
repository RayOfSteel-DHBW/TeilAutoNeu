# Impressum (Legal Notice) Requirements  
In Germany (and EU law by country-of-origin), nearly all commercial websites must publish an **Impressum** (imprint) listing the operator’s identity.  Under the current Digitales-Dienste-Gesetz (DDG, which replaced TMG in May 2024) § 5, the imprint must include the provider’s full name and legal form, full postal address, and contact details (email and phone)【7†L88-L96】.  For legal entities, this includes the registered name, legal structure (e.g. “GmbH”), representative person, capital info, and registration numbers【7†L88-L96】.  If regulated activities apply, add the supervising authority; if company is in a register (Handelsregister, Vereinsregister, etc.), list it and its number【7†L88-L96】.  VAT or economic ID numbers must also be given if held【7†L88-L96】.  These rules essentially mirror the old TMG obligations and implement the EU E‑Commerce Directive’s transparency rules.  (Also note: separate B2B rules under the Dienstleistungs-Informationspflichten-Verordnung (DL-InfoV) may mandate similar data, and local press laws may demand a “Verantwortlicher” in some cases.)  

# Datenschutzerklärung (Privacy Policy) Contents  
The privacy policy must fulfil GDPR transparency requirements (Articles 12–14).  Article 13 GDPR lists all mandatory disclosures: you must name the *controller* (with contact info) and any DPO, state each processing purpose and legal basis, any legitimate interests, all recipient categories, third-country transfers (and safeguards), retention periods, and all data subject rights (access, correction, deletion, objection, etc.)【11†L168-L177】【11†L188-L197】.  In practice this means your policy should clearly identify your organization (name, address, email) as data controller and DPO contact (if any)【40†L342-L350】.  It should then describe what personal data you collect (e.g. log data, cookies), why (marketing, analytics, transaction, etc.), the legal basis (e.g. Art.6(1)(b), (f) or consent), and how long data are kept【40†L389-L397】.  Finally include all statutorily required notices (e.g. right to withdraw consent, lodge a complaint).  German supervisory authorities even publish model texts – for example the North-Rhine Westphalia DPA’s website provides a sample notice showing these sections formatted under “Art.13 DS-GVO” headings【40†L342-L350】【40†L389-L397】.  

# Analytics and Cookie Consent (GDPR / ePrivacy)  
EU law (ePrivacy Directive Art.5(3)) and the German TTDSG/TDDDG now require **prior opt‑in consent** for any non-essential cookies or equivalent tracking【20†L64-L72】【18†L153-L162】.  In short, all analytics, marketing or “performance” cookies are non-technical and may not be placed before the user consents【20†L64-L72】【20†L99-L104】.  Even “cookie-less” analytics tools that do not store browser cookies but still process device data (e.g. IP addresses, fingerprints) fall under the same rule: they access information on the user’s device, so consent is generally required【18†L156-L164】【18†L163-L172】.  Recent German guidance and case law emphasize this strict stance.  For example, a 2023 German administrative court held that Google Tag Manager (which handles analytics scripts and captures IPs) triggers TTDSG’s rules and cannot run before the user explicitly opts in【18†L153-L162】.  The court noted that GTM processes personal data like IP addresses and that such processing (outside of strictly-necessary functions) demands GDPR consent【18†L163-L172】.  In practice, therefore, **even privacy-friendly analytics (e.g. Matomo/Plausible with no cookies or anonymized IP) should be treated as non-essential tracking**.  They will still require an opt-in banner or equivalent, unless it is demonstrably limited to strictly necessary service functions (a high bar under German law)【18†L153-L162】【20†L99-L104】.  (By contrast, truly essential cookies—login sessions, shopping cart, consent state itself—are exempt.) 

# Consent Management and Banner Usage  
Under current guidance, a consent banner or management tool is virtually mandatory for any analytics beyond the absolutely essential.  German data protection authorities (DSK) stress that consent must be obtained via **clear affirmative action**【13†L179-L184】【22†L196-L204】.  Implied consent or merely continuing to browse is not valid【22†L188-L197】.  Likewise, pre-checked boxes or “accept” buttons without a clear opt-out are invalid.  Consent must be granular (separate options for analytics vs marketing, etc.) and revocable as easily as given【13†L179-L184】【22†L188-L197】.  In summary: you should block all analytics scripts by default and only activate them if and when the user actively consents.  Case law (as noted above) confirms cookie banners are no longer optional in Germany【18†L153-L162】【22†L196-L204】. 

# Local SEO: Business Name and Address  
For search/listing purposes, always use the **official registered name** of the business (including exact legal suffix, e.g. “GmbH” or “e.K.”) as it appears in the commercial register.  The address should be in standard German format: the company name on the first line, street name and house number on the second, then postal code and city on the third【41†L254-L262】.  (For example:  
```
Acme GmbH  
Musterstraße 12  
12345 Berlin
```  
) This ensures consistency with official records (Handelsregister, tax office) and avoids confusion.  If your business serves a specific region, state that explicitly (e.g. “Service area: Berlin metropolitan region”).  Avoid using alternate transliterations or abbreviations.  These details should match the “customer-approved business facts” exactly.  (In Germany, the **Geschäftsanschrift** must be a physical address where the company can be reached and is publicly listed【26†L179-L187】.) 

# Media and Copyright Audit  
All planned images, videos, and other media must be vetted for copyright.  Identify each asset’s source and license.  If an image is under a Creative Commons (CC) license, comply fully: for CC BY (attribution) licenses you must give “appropriate credit” (author name), link to the license, and note any changes【43†L79-L82】.  The Potsdam University Press guidance notes that “authors must be credited and cited correctly in every use”【44†L299-L304】.  CC0 or public-domain works have no attribution requirement (but you may still credit for courtesy).  Any stock or third-party media require a valid commercial license or permission; keep copies of the license or invoice.  Document each file (e.g. filename, author, license type, rights holder) in an audit log.  For images under CC BY or CC BY-SA, include an attribution credit in the footer or caption (e.g. “Photo by Jane Doe, CC BY 4.0”).  For proprietary stock, ensure your license covers web use and note any “no-derivatives” restrictions.  The goal is that every media item on the site has clear provenance and any necessary credit text in place, to satisfy legal and attribution obligations【43†L79-L82】【44†L299-L304】.
